STOP 02 / FIRST EXERCISE

Make the numbers
visible.

Explore sample size and statistical power with a graph, using only a prompt in Copilot.

IN THIS STOP

First exercise

The scenario

A team is planning a hypothetical randomized trial with a continuous outcome. It expects a standard deviation of 10 points but is uncertain about the treatment effect. Explore differences of 2, 3, and 4 points.

1. Generate a power graph

Start a new Copilot chat and try this prompt:

PROMPT FOR COPILOT

We are planning a two-arm randomized trial with equal group sizes and a continuous outcome. Assume normally distributed outcomes with a standard deviation of 10 points in both groups. Use an analytical power calculation for a two-sided independent-samples t-test at a significance level of 0.05. Plot power against sample size from 20 to 500 participants per group, with separate curves for differences between group means of 2, 3, and 4 points. Add a horizontal line at 80% power. Label the axes clearly and briefly explain the graph and assumptions.

Look at the result

  • Did Copilot generate a graph with all three requested curves and the 80% reference line?
  • Are the axis labels and legend clear and easy to read?
  • Does the explanation help you understand what is shown in the graph?

2. Explore the assumptions

Continue in the same chat. Try one or both follow-up prompts and see how Copilot changes the graph.

PROMPT FOR COPILOT

How many participants per group are needed for 80% power for each of the three differences? Give the total for the trial too, and mark these points on the graph.

PROMPT FOR COPILOT

How does the graph change if the standard deviation is 12 rather than 10 points? Keep the other assumptions the same and show the original and new curves together so I can compare them.

Then try a question of your own, or ask Copilot to make the graph easier to read.

3. Discuss

  • How did changing the assumed effect or standard deviation change the required sample size?
  • Did Copilot explain its assumptions clearly?
  • Did the follow-up prompts produce the changes you expected?
